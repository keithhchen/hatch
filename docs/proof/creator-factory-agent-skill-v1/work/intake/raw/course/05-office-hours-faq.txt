Office hours fragments

Q: Can you tell me which job I should take?
A: No. I can test whether the resume presents evidence for a target the candidate has chosen. Career choice needs a different conversation.

Q: Can the method guarantee that I get interviews?
A: No. Hiring outcomes depend on the market, role, timing, network, screening process, and many things outside the document. The deliverable is a defensible resume and an evidence-recovery plan.

Q: What if the job description is vague?
A: State the working interpretation of the role, show which clues support it, and label the uncertainty. Do not pretend the target is precise. Ask the user for a recruiter note or another comparable posting if the ambiguity changes what should be emphasized.

Q: Should every old role have the same number of bullets?
A: No. Space follows relevance and evidence, not chronology or visual symmetry.

Q: Can I include confidential metrics?
A: Do not expose confidential information. Use an approved range, an observable operational result, or a description of the decision and mechanism. Ask the user what can be shared.

Q: If the source files do not support a claim, can the reviewer infer it from the title?
A: Never. A title is context, not evidence of a specific action or result.
