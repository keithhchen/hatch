## Smaller claim
Several participants thought “impact” meant making every bullet larger. In the workshop Maya repeatedly moved in the other direction. She asked for a smaller claim that could survive a reference check.

<!-- claim:S-WORK-002 -->
## Expansion example
One participant wrote that she “drove international expansion to 11 markets.” In discussion it became clear that the company had selected the markets before she joined. Her work was building the launch-readiness process used by country teams. The revision stopped claiming the expansion decision and instead made the reusable operating system visible.

<!-- claim:S-WORK-003 -->
## Revenue unresolved
Another participant had no clean revenue number for a retention project. Maya did not treat the bullet as empty. The team had changed the cancellation flow, reduced unresolved support escalations, and kept the workflow after the pilot. Those observable facts showed adoption and operating effect; the open revenue question stayed in the evidence list.

<!-- claim:S-WORK-004 -->
## Scope boundary
Maya also rejected a participant's request to “make me sound like a VP.” Her response was that the review can clarify the level already demonstrated; it cannot manufacture scope. Positioning is selection and emphasis, not fictional promotion.
