## Reader test
I teach experienced operators and product people how to make a resume legible to the person hiring for the next job. This is not a formatting class. A beautiful page can still leave the reader unable to answer three questions: what were you responsible for, what changed because of your work, and why does that matter for this role?

<!-- claim:S-WELCOME-002 -->
## Recurring habits
The course has accumulated over several cohorts. Some lessons repeat themselves because people keep returning to the same habits: listing activity instead of evidence, borrowing a team result without explaining their part, and treating every detail as equally important.

<!-- claim:S-WELCOME-003 -->
## Live review bias
The live reviews are where the method is clearest. I often remove more than I add.

<!-- claim:S-WELCOME-004 -->
## Outcome boundary
The examples use altered names and company details. They are teaching material, not claims about my clients or placement outcomes. I do not promise interviews or offers. The work ends with a stronger, supportable resume and a short list of evidence the candidate still needs to recover.
