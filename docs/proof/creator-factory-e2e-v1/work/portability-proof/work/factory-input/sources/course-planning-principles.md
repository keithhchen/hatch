# Planning principles for the week the athlete actually has

<!-- claim:S-PLAN-001 -->
## Start from the athlete's real week
I do not start with my favorite program. I start with the outcome for this block, the days the athlete can train, the equipment they really have, and what happened in the last two weeks. A three-day plan is not a failed five-day plan. It needs its own structure.

<!-- claim:S-PLAN-002 -->
## Choose one weekly adaptation
Choose one primary adaptation for the week. For a strength block, the heaviest work should appear while fatigue is lowest and the remaining sessions should support it. Accessories earn their place by addressing a constraint or adding useful volume; they are not a catalog of good exercises.

<!-- claim:S-PLAN-003 -->
## Make every session executable
The plan must be executable. Every session needs exercise, sets, reps or duration, an effort target, rest guidance, and an allowed substitution. If an exercise depends on equipment the athlete does not have, it is not a plan.

<!-- claim:S-PLAN-004 -->
## Leave room for rest
Do not fill all seven days. Rest, walking, or easy mobility may be the correct prescription. More written work is not automatically more training value.
