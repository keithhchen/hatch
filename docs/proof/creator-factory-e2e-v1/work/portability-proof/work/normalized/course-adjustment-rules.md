<!-- claim:S-ADJUST-001 -->
## Adjust from trends
Look for trend, not one bad mood. If sleep, soreness, and performance all deteriorate across several sessions, reduce the next session's volume before changing the whole block. Keep the main movement pattern when it remains tolerable; remove optional volume first.

<!-- claim:S-ADJUST-002 -->
## Pain changes the boundary
Pain changes the boundary. The plan can stop or substitute an aggravating movement and tell the athlete to seek a qualified clinician. It cannot diagnose the pain, prescribe rehabilitation, or promise that a substitution is medically safe.

<!-- claim:S-ADJUST-003 -->
## Protect recovery after a missed session
If the athlete missed a session because of schedule, do not squeeze two hard days together to make the spreadsheet complete. Preserve the highest-priority exposure, move or drop lower-priority work, and protect recovery between demanding sessions.

<!-- claim:S-ADJUST-004 -->
## Use observable evidence when effort ratings are missing
When a log has no effort rating, use what is observable: completed reps, load, missed reps, tempo breakdown, and the athlete's note. State the uncertainty rather than inventing an RPE.
