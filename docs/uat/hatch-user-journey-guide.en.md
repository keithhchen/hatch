# Turn Experience into Results

Hatch helps you turn what you know, what you have done, and what you are willing to share into help that someone else can use.

You do not need to configure an Agent or understand models and internal systems. You only need to say what you want to help someone achieve, give Hatch the important material, add your judgment, and make the decisions that matter.

## Why Hatch is designed this way

Hatch is not built around checking off features. It is built around helping a real person get a real result. That leads to a few simple principles:

- **Start with the person’s goal**: people are not trying to create an object, run a workflow, or upload a file. They are trying to accomplish something real and take away a result they can use.
- **Keep complexity inside Hatch**: the system may be complex, but the path should be clear. You express intent, provide important context, and make the meaningful choices; Hatch organizes the complicated work.
- **Respect the judgment inside experience**: material is more than text. Why a method works, when it should not be copied, and which boundaries matter are equally important.
- **Make the result usable**: the result should not merely be generated. It should be understandable, reviewable, editable, shareable, or ready to deliver.
- **Reduce the cost carried by the user**: fewer guesses, less repeated input, and fewer unnecessary settings leave your attention for the decisions that actually matter.

Hatch is therefore not a toolbox full of options. It is a path from experience, material, and intent to a result.

## Find your path

- **Creator**: you have a method, case, or body of experience and want to turn it into a Product that others can use.
- **Coding Agent user**: you want to bring your own Agent work, files on your computer, or pages you are authorized to access into a Product.
- **Buyer**: you have found a Product and want to use it to solve a problem and get a result.

## Key Concepts

- **Product**: content and methods organized around a clear promise to help someone do a particular kind of work.
- **Creator**: the person who provides the method, experience, and boundaries.
- **Buyer**: the person who uses the Product for help.
- **About You**: the conversation Hatch uses to understand a Creator’s method, judgment, and boundaries.
- **Review**: the part where the Creator and Hatch check whether the result follows that method.
- **Brief**: the goal and context a Buyer provides before starting a particular piece of work.

## 1. If you are a Creator

### 1. Start with what you want to help someone achieve

Sign in to your Creator account and create a Product with a name and a promise:

- **Name**: make it immediately clear what the Product can help someone do.
- **Promise**: use plain language to say what someone will be able to achieve with it.

For example:

> Help creators turn scattered experience into answers they can act on and reuse.

The promise is not a technical description or a feature list. It should describe the progress a person actually wants to make.

### 2. Files: give Hatch the material you are willing to use

You can upload articles, notes, cases, video transcripts, or method documents directly. You can also import material from a Coding Agent; these are parallel sources, and both become material for the same Product.

Before adding material, confirm that you own it or have the right to use it, that it helps someone understand your method, and that you are willing to use it in this Product.

![Creator Files page](screenshots/creator-files-real-uat.png)

### 3. About You: explain what matters most

Hatch uses the material to understand your method. Answer in the same way you would speak to a colleague or client:

- What problem does your method really solve?
- Which judgments matter most?
- Which situations should not be copied directly?
- What makes an answer meet your standard?

This is not a request to copy your material again. It is a way to make the experience, trade-offs, and boundaries behind the material clear.

![Creator About You page](screenshots/creator-about-you-real-uat.png)

### 4. Review: make sure the result feels like your method

Review places the Product in concrete situations so you can see how it responds. Look for:

- Whether it addresses the actual problem.
- Whether it applies your method correctly.
- Whether it crosses a boundary.
- What should change if it is not right.

You can accept the result or point out what needs to change. Meaningful changes create a new version while keeping the work you have already done.

### 5. Brief: make it easy for a Buyer to begin

The Brief is the set of questions a Buyer answers before starting a particular piece of work, such as their goal, current situation, constraints, or deadline.

Make questions that affect the result required and less important questions optional. Questions should be easy for a Buyer to answer, not require them to understand your internal process.

### 6. Decide when to publish

When you are ready for other people to use the Product, you decide whether to publish it. Publishing means you are willing for Buyers to start with the promise and method you have prepared.

## 2. Import material from a Coding Agent

Coding Agent import is another Product material source alongside manual upload. It is useful for content you have already reviewed and approved.

### What you can import

1. **Chat work from the Coding Agent itself**: conversations from Agents such as Codex, Claude, OpenClaw, or WorkBuddy that contain your own work, methods, cases, and judgments.
2. **Files on your computer**: notes, documents, drafts, cases, or other material you have approved.
3. **Pages you can access in your browser**: your own social media, content dashboards, work back offices, or other pages available after you sign in.

Hatch uses only the content you explicitly select and are authorized to use. You can preview it before deciding which Product should receive it.

### The normal user flow

1. Click **Connect Hatch** or **Authorize** in your Coding Agent.
2. Confirm that the browser shows your Creator account and the Product file permission.
3. Approve the connection; authorization completes automatically and returns to the Coding Agent.
4. Select the content, preview it, and confirm what you want to bring in.
5. Choose the destination Product and upload it.
6. Open the Product’s Files page to confirm that the material is there.

You do not need to copy an authorization code, run commands, or handle a token. Before uploading, confirm only the ownership of the content, the preview, and the destination Product.

![Coding Agent authorization page](screenshots/context-intake-oauth-consent-20260819.png)

## 3. If you are a Buyer

### 1. Understand the Product’s promise first

Open the Product link shared by the Creator. Before you begin, you should be able to understand:

- Who made the Product.
- What it promises to help you do.
- What context you need to provide.

If the promise does not match your problem, do not force it. Find help that fits the situation better.

![Buyer Product detail page: confirm the promise, boundaries, and access](screenshots/buyer-product-detail-real.png)

When it fits, choose **Get access**. If the Product needs to be added to your account, Hatch asks you to confirm the Product and release first:

- The Product name and Creator are correct.
- You are receiving the current approved release.
- The access is permanent, and whether payment is required.

![Buyer access confirmation page: confirm the Product and release](screenshots/buyer-checkout-confirm-real.png)

After you choose **Add to my account**, access is recorded on your account. The success page gives you the next step: open Desktop, view the order receipt, or view access details.

![Buyer access success page: open Desktop or view access details](screenshots/buyer-order-success-real.png)

![Buyer has Product access](screenshots/buyer-entitlement-success-20260819.png)

### 2. Answer the starting questions

Only answer what matters for this piece of work. Required questions help the Product understand your goal; optional questions are useful only when they can improve the result.

### 3. Let the Product help with the work

After you provide the context, the Product applies the Creator’s method to your problem. Pay attention to whether the result helps with the situation at hand and whether you need to add or adjust anything.

![Buyer has a result](screenshots/buyer-task-complete-20260819.png)

### 4. Bring the result back into your work

A good result should not have to stay inside Hatch. You should be able to keep reading, editing, sharing, submitting, or using it as the starting point for the next step.

## 4. One minute before you begin

### Creator

- State the result you want to help someone achieve in one sentence.
- Provide only material you are willing and authorized to deliver.
- Explain the method, judgment, and boundaries in your own words.
- Use Review to check whether the result genuinely feels like your method.
- Write the questions a Buyer truly needs to answer.
- Decide when you are ready for other people to begin.

### Coding Agent user

- Select only chat, files, or pages you have reviewed and approved.
- Preview before confirming the import.
- Confirm the destination Product.
- Open Product Files after import to see the result.

### Buyer

- Read the Product’s promise first.
- Provide only the information this piece of work really needs.
- Decide whether the result genuinely helps you take the next step.

## Finally

Hatch is valuable not because it gives you more features, but because it makes it easier to start from your own experience and goal and reach a result that is trustworthy, understandable, and ready to use.

Do not ask only, “Does the feature exist?” Ask the more important question:

> Is the user more likely to succeed because of it?
